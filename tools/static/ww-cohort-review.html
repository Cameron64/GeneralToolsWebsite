Cohort review · where the leak actually is

A cohort is one group of people who joined around the same time. The cockpit (page 2) watches a group while it is still moving - and cannot see the leak yet, because nobody is lost until day 90. This page looks back at groups that have finished, where the loss is real and final: the path step by step, and the same steps group over group. This is what the body looks at when deciding what to change for the next group - it shapes the decision, it does not make it. There are no individual people on this page at all, by design.

Sight, not direction. This page shows where the chapter loses people and whether that is getting better. It records what the body changed and what moved next to it. It never says "do this." Reading the shape and deciding the next move belongs to the membership body.

Finished groups only. The leak is a final fact, so it is only drawn for groups past day 90. A group that is still live appears here greyed and unreadable - its numbers are not final and would lie if charted as a leak.

Oriented → first GBM is where the chapter loses the most people.
Count: across Winter, Summer and Fall, this step lost more people than any other - by both headcount and rate - the escort step, the hard weeks after orientation before a newcomer has made a friend. Counter-metric: it is also the step improving fastest (40% → 47% → 54%), so it is worth more attention, not less. Measured against: the body's failure line X unset by body.
This is the step the data points to. Whether to act on it - and how (more buddies, a different orientation night, slower intake) - is the body's call. Echo shows that this is the step and remembers what happens when you act; it does not say what to do. And "biggest drop" is not the same as "easiest to move."

Why this page exists
Frame Two clocks · this is the closed clock
  • The cockpit reads a live group: a snapshot and an aging signal, no leak. This page reads finished groups, where the leak is a final fact.
  • That split is the answer to "where is the steering wheel." It was never on the live page - the live page can't see the leak. It is here, on the looking-back page, and it shapes the next group.
Design No people on this page
  • Every row here is a step or a group, never a person. There is nothing to click that touches an individual.
  • That is what keeps it from becoming a chase queue. It steers process, so it structurally cannot manufacture per-person follow-up. Person-level work stays bounded on the cockpit's four writes.
Guardrail Sight, not direction
  • The page names the biggest drop and shows the trend. It does not say "recruit buddies this week."
  • The learning ledger is the strongest steering element and the easiest to over-read: it is correlation recorded, not causation claimed. The body reads it and decides.
Order Why this sequence
  • Eligibility first, so it is impossible to mistake a live group for a readable leak.
  • The funnel, then the one step named - so the biggest drop is earned from the picture, not asserted.
  • Trend, then the ledger - the leak's direction before the changes that might explain it.
  • Body-owned params last - the thresholds the whole page measures against, shown unset.
Absent by design
No "do this next" or any recommendation - the page names the step and stops. No per-person row, queue, or inbox anywhere. No causal claim drawn from the ledger's correlation. No leak on a live group. No blended health score that hides the three numbers per group. No "mark as member" - dues stays read-only from Action Network everywhere in Echo.
