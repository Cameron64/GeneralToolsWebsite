Echo · warm welcome flow · low-fidelity journey map

One Echo Person, four doors · oriented, then escorted

The system of record for the funnel is Echo - anyone can have an Echo Person record (and an Echo account) regardless of membership, and most people start here as non-members. Welcome Home writes the name, email, and phone to Echo. One Echo Person per human (keyed by email), one cohort; four doors create or confirm it. Action Network is a separate side system - the record for membership and dues only - that Echo reads by email, never writes. Today, any door that establishes contact without a membership is invisible to us, and any membership preceded by a newsletter signup is also invisible. That is the hole this redesign closes.

The spine. Every door funnels toward the same recurring virtual orientation ("I belong to a chapter"), framed around the person's actual fight - not bureaucratic onboarding. Their Rose Buddy optionally joins that orientation, but definitely escorts them, in person, to their first GBM - the belonging beat where the warm welcome lands or fails.

We design this · Screen 1
Welcome Home
A named Rose Buddy first, then the one dated orientation (virtual), then the RSVP. Framed around their fight ("come meet the folks fighting your rent and utility hikes"), not bureaucratic onboarding. Double duty: Door 1's capture surface AND the landing page the text link points to for every door.
Cadence reminder
Sends a reminder. Flags a contact if nothing has happened.
Logs attendance
Logs that they attended the orientation - the funnel target every door pointed at.
Cadence ladder + GBM check-in
A 4-week ladder: a text, then calls. A "stalled at 30 days" flag if there is no engagement. Check-in logs that they showed at the GBM.
Reads dues back from AN
Dues are recorded on Action Network - the only contact-to-member line. The Echo Person stays the system of record for the human and the funnel; Echo reads the dues fact back from Action Network by email and flips them to "member." Echo reads dues; it never writes them.
The Rose Buddy
Front-line volunteer · one named person, their own two or three · page 4 desk
Auto-assigned at capture
Ordinary case: the newcomer is auto-matched to an under-cap buddy, load-balanced across the pool. No one picks - it just happens.
A person steps in only for the exceptions below - never to work a roster of every newcomer.
⎇ Branch · pool full, no buddy free yet
They wait in a visible, honestly-labeled queue - not a fake promise of a buddy. The signal to recruit more buddies, never to overload the ones we have.
The named face on Welcome Home
Appears by name as the person the newcomer will meet, then opens a first note - who they are, what they are fighting for.
NotesShared org knowledge - they travel with the relationship if the buddy ever changes, and leadership can read them during a re-placement. Shared with the buddy-and-leadership circle, not an org-wide dossier.
The human behind the cadence
The reminders are automated. The buddy is the person, not the reminder - a real check-in, not a nudge.
⎇ Branch · buddy steps away gracefully
A handoff from their own desk - pick a successor or ask the pool to cover, notes and all. Handled at the desk, no escalation.
⎇ Branch · buddy vanishes (ungraceful)
Ghosts, is removed, or leaves with no handoff. A person re-places them with a new buddy, notes intact - so the newcomer never resets to zero. Continuity of care, not a restart.
Optionally joins orientation
A friendly named face in the virtual room, if they can make it. Optional - not the commitment.
◆ The hard commitment
Escorts them to the first GBM, in person
The one thing the buddy definitely does. A room of strangers becomes "someone is here for me."
DistinctionThe escort happens HERE, as a real act. The cockpit only reads it back later from the two of them signing in together - it is not a logged chase task.
Keeps the relationship
Supports the in-person join ask off the back of belonging - never before it - and stays the human thread afterward.
When the person reaches active, the rose rolls off (read from Action Network, never marked by hand) and frees a slot back to the pool.

Leadership has no lane here by design. It tends the pool and reviews cohorts on its own cadence - not along one newcomer's timeline - so its views live on their own screens: the Cadence cockpit (page 2), Cohort review (page 3), and Pool & program (page 5). The only points where it touches a single person's journey are the two exception branches above - a full pool, and a buddy who leaves without a handoff.
