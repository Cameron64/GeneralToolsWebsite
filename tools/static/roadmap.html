The architecture, in one line

How the pieces fit together, without the jargon.

Echo (the chapter's own tool) gets a new member_data section, running on DigitalOcean with its own database. Action Network, the chapter's national database, stays the master copy - Echo only reads from it, through a locked one-way door. Every time someone looks at a member's details, that look gets written down, and viewing details requires a second login step. Backups are locked with a key only the steward holds, and stored off any one person's laptop.

Two tracks, moving at the same time

Building the tool and getting the chapter's blessing happen in parallel. Both have to finish before the pilot can start.

Build track

- the software, in five phases
Phase 0Now

The spike

What it does: A short, free test. Cam runs code that already exists against Action Network to check three membership facts and try one tag update.

Why it matters: It answers one question before any money is spent: can we trust Action Network's own membership status, or do we need the weekly file instead?

Unblocks: which data source Phase 2 is built on.
Gated on: Cam getting access to the Action Network login key.
Phase 1Next

Infrastructure

What it does: Moves Echo, the chapter's tool, off a volunteer's personal laptop onto a real host (DigitalOcean), with its own managed database.

Why it matters: Right now, if that laptop breaks or the volunteer leaves, the chapter's tool goes with it. This makes the tool the chapter's, not one person's.

Unblocks: Phase 2 - there is nowhere to put the data until this database exists.
Gated on: the Leadership Committee's yes and budget.
Phase 2Planned

Data tier

What it does: Builds the actual record-keeping: weekly snapshots of who is a member, a bridge from the old spreadsheet, and a live, read-only link to Action Network.

Why it matters: This is what lets us see who joins, who stays, and who drifts - a history Action Network alone cannot show.

Unblocks: Phase 3, which is built on top of this data.
Gated on: Phase 1's database, plus the spike's answer on which data source to trust.
Phase 3Planned

The Echo app

What it does: Adds the part people actually use: member records, roles, a simple ladder showing how engaged someone is, a log of who looked at what, and a required second login step for anyone viewing member details.

Why it matters: This is where staff and volunteers see and use the data - safely, with every look recorded.

Unblocks: Phase 4's controls, which operate on these records.
Gated on: Phase 2's data tier.
Phase 4Planned

Custody & governance

What it does: Adds the safety layer: nightly encrypted backups stored off any one laptop, rules for deleting old data, regular key rotation, and a written checklist showing the chapter follows national's data rules.

Why it matters: This turns "we hold people's data" into "we are careful, accountable stewards of people's data."

Unblocks: the pilot - it cannot start without this in place.
Gated on: Phases 2 and 3 existing to protect.

Governance track

- the chapter's decisions, in two meetings
Meeting 1Now

Leadership Committee meeting

What it does: A short meeting where the chapter's leaders hear the plan, see the real cost, and decide whether to fund it and name Cam the IT Coordinator - the chapter's named data steward.

Why it matters: Nothing here can spend money or touch real member data without this yes.

Unblocks: Phase 1's spending.
Gated on: nothing - it's already scheduled for next week.
Meeting 2Planned

Adoption session

What it does: A follow-up meeting that works through nine short motions to formally adopt the chapter's written data-handling policy - who can see what, how long we keep it, what happens if something ever leaks.

Why it matters: This is where the chapter agrees to the rules the tool will enforce - not just the budget, the actual rules.

Unblocks: a pilot that runs under a chapter-approved policy, not just Cam's judgment.
Gated on: Meeting 1 happening first.
Convergence pointPlanned

Pilot: one cohort, one neighborhood

What it does: Try the whole system on one small group - roughly 25 to 50 people, in one cohort (a group that joined around the same time) and one neighborhood group. MEC (the membership committee) and the Neighborhood Groups committee pick which.

Why it matters: Real numbers and real hours worked, on a small, reversible slice - before this touches the whole chapter's data.

Unblocks: everything that follows.
Gated on: both tracks finishing - Phase 4 on the build side, the adoption session on the governance side.
+90 daysPlanned

Day-90 readout

What it does: Ninety days into the pilot, Cam reports back to the Leadership Committee: how many hours it took, what the numbers showed, and whether it's worth scaling up.

Why it matters: This is the chapter's checkpoint to say yes, fix something, or stop - before more people's data is involved.

BeyondPlanned

Scale & build on top

What it does: If the pilot earns it: scale to all four neighborhood groups the chapter already voted to create, build the warm-welcome and Rose Buddy features on top of the data, and revisit adopting National's own tool if they ever offer one (around January).

Why it matters: The pilot proves the foundation holds weight before we build the rest of the house on it.
