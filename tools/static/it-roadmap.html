Austin DSA · Information Technology Sub-Committee

What we are building, and in what order

A roadmap for this term. Written so that the people who sit in these chairs next inherit a plan and a foundation, rather than a folder of passwords and somebody's memory.

Term: 2026 to 2027 Parent committee: Logistics & Planning First meeting: 20 August 2026 Status: draft for the sub-committee to adopt

The short version. The chapter's systems run today on the care and memory of a handful of generous people who kept things going through a quiet stretch and never asked for the job. That is a debt, not a scandal.

This term's work is turning that goodwill into institutions: a written record of what we run, access that can be handed over and handed back, backups we have actually tested, and a committee that outlives every one of us, including the people carrying it now, who deserve to be able to get busy without the lights going out.

01Why this matters beyond the tech

Two reasons, and neither of them is "the tools are annoying".

The chapter already voted for it

Organising by Neighborhood Groups requires secure handling of member information, lists that belong to each neighborhood, and access that rotates safely as volunteers come and go. Nobody currently owns making that possible. Under our bylaws, that is this sub-committee. The inventory and the access work are not housekeeping; they are the prerequisites for something the chapter has already decided to do.

Continuity is the actual product

Our bylaws ask this sub-committee to keep an inventory, to manage permissions, to educate members, and to make sure access survives a change of seat. Every one of those is really the same request: do not let the chapter's ability to function depend on one person being free this month.

02The roadmap

Four phases. The first is about people, not technology, because nothing else is possible without it. Each phase assumes the one before it landed.

Now
Constitute
August to September. Become a real committee.
  • Three or more members on a written roster
  • Elect a second co-chair, so no single person is the whole committee
  • Fill the roles that need no special access, each with a named backup
  • Agree how decisions get made and write it down
  • Set a monthly cadence and stick to it
Next
Know
September to November. Write down what we actually run.
  • Finish the inventory: every system, who holds it, who pays for it
  • Clear every unconfirmed entry rather than one a month
  • Agree that access requests come through this sub-committee and get recorded
  • Bring the Treasurer a real picture of what our software line buys
  • Publish it all on the wiki, so the next person starts where we finished
Then
Protect
Autumn onward. Look after our people and our data.
  • Revise and deliver the digital security teach-in for members
  • Practical focus: making organisers harder to target online
  • Contribute a data-custody view to the chapter's technology policy work
  • Written guidance for handling member information, in line with national's
  • Prove our backups restore, rather than assuming they would
Direction
Endure
Multi-term. The part we prepare rather than finish.
  • Move toward single sign-on, so joining and leaving are one action
  • Keycloak is our chosen destination for this
  • Prefer joining an existing service over running our own
  • Build everything from now on so that move is a swap, not a rewrite
  • Documentation and handover as a habit, not an end-of-term scramble

04The one long-term decision, made early

Most of what makes handover painful comes down to identity: who you are, and what that entitles you to. Get that right and adding or removing someone is a single action. Get it wrong and every system is its own separate chore, which is roughly where most chapters live.

So we are naming the destination now, even though we are not building it this year: single sign-on, with Keycloak as the chosen provider. Deciding early is nearly free and changes how we build today; deciding late is expensive and changes nothing.

An honest unknown. We would much rather join an existing service than run our own, because operating one is a standing commitment that has to survive people getting busy. We have heard second hand that national is moving in the same direction, but that is a long way from an offer to host anyone, and to our knowledge nobody has actually asked.

Finding out is on our list, and it belongs to whoever takes the inter-chapter role. Until we have an answer, we plan as though we would be doing it ourselves.

Getting involved

You do not need to be technical. Most of this work is asking people questions, writing answers down, and following through.

  • Just be a member. No job, no attendance requirement, no hours. Right now it is the most useful thing anyone can do
  • Take a role if one interests you. Every one is written to fit a busy person and every one has a second person on it
  • Take a backup slot if you would rather learn something before owning it. That is a completely normal way in
  • Come and disagree. A committee that only hears from its chairs makes worse decisions
