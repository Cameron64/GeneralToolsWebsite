Neighborhoods · where members get rooted

After orientation and a first citywide GBM, a member needs a local, lower-stakes home - their Neighborhood Group. This screen is the organizing spine: the four neighborhoods the chapter voted to stand up in R2026-05-01, each shown by its health - members assigned, whether it has a recognized steering committee, recent events, and local capacity. A neighborhood is a container (it houses people, with no clock); the cohort is the instrument (a clock, used to measure). They share one record, so the same data reads either way.

The secure home the resolution already requires. R2026-05-01 orders the chapter to build neighborhood rolls, a steering-committee contact directory, documentarian event logs, and access rights that rotate yearly - and to "establish rules on proper and secure handling of member data." Today that would live as open Google Drive sheets. Echo holds it on a chapter-controlled store off Google (a DigitalOcean-hosted or self-hosted database, in line with the chapter's divestment), access-controlled and audited. Those four data jobs are not new scope - the chapter already voted for them; the capacity, escort and rooting views below are where the same store could grow next.

Sight, not direction - and access-scoped on purpose. There is no neighborhood leaderboard; a thin chamber reads as a risk to staff, never a failing grade. A central map of where members live is genuinely sensitive, so it is scoped to that neighborhood's own stewards and logged. The rolls and the map are mandated either way by the resolution - the real choice is an open Google Sheet (or no safe map at all) versus an access-scoped, audited store with fewer eyes. This is the more private option, not a new exposure.

Every name, count and percentage on this screen is illustrative - sample data to show the shape of the view, not real members or real readings.

6Absent by design · what this screen deliberately does not do
Held back on purpose
No neighborhood leaderboard or ranking. No "assign every member to a chamber and walk away" - a dead chamber is flagged as a risk before anyone is routed into it. No forcing a buddy's capacity up; leadership can ask, only the buddy raises their own ceiling. No member-level map exposed beyond a neighborhood's own stewards. No "mark as member" - good standing is read from Action Network. No day-90 number on a live cohort.
Why it is shaped this way
Frame Container vs instrument
  • A neighborhood houses people - it is durable and has no clock. A cohort observes - it has a clock and ages out. This screen is the container; the cockpit and cohort review are the instrument.
  • They are not two ways to slice one list. Treating them as interchangeable is the category error to avoid: the neighborhood must not inherit the cohort's clock, and the cohort must not try to be a home.
  • But they share one record, which is what makes Region 4's cross-query possible.
Risk A dead chamber is worse
  • Neighborhoods are not free retention. An understaffed chamber absorbs a newcomer and then strands them - worse than the old single GBM, which at least existed.
  • So the screen's first job is to make per-chamber health visible (Really South, shown thin) - never to quietly route people into a dead end.
Rule Capacity is non-fungible
  • Once the destination is "your neighborhood meeting," the cup is four pools, not one. You cannot ship a North buddy to root a Really South newcomer.
  • So capacity is read per chamber (Region 2). A starved chamber can hide inside a healthy chapter total.
Design Escort vs rooting
  • The buddy's two jobs split: escort (geography-free - orientation + first GBM) and rooting (geography-bound - handoff to a local contact).
  • The buddy system gains a stage rather than breaking. Echo holds both relationships on the record.
Answer The surveillance question
  • A central map of where members live is genuinely sensitive. Do not wave it away.
  • The honest answer: the rolls and map are mandated either way by the resolution - the only choice is open Google Sheets vs an access-scoped, audited store with fewer eyes. This is the more private option.
Absent by design
No leaderboard ranking the four neighborhoods against each other. No chase queue of people who left a thin chamber - the response is a process change (stand up the committee), never a row action. No member-level location data outside a neighborhood's own stewards. No blended "neighborhood score" that hides the honest thin-chamber flag. No day-90 verdict on a live cohort.
