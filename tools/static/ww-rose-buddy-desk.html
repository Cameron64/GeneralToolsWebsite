Rose Buddy desk · your own few

This is the exact inverse of the Cadence cockpit. Same underlying data, opposite slice. The cockpit deliberately shows leadership no individual people - that was the whole anti-chase-queue discipline. This desk is nothing but people, but only the two or three who are yours. A Rose Buddy is a volunteer welcomer; this screen tells them who to meet, when and where, and what they know about them - and lets them jot a note, hand someone off when life happens, and step in and out of the pool. It is phone-first: a buddy reads it walking into a meeting. There are no rates and no percentages anywhere, and a buddy never sees anyone else's roster.

Your few, never everyone. This desk is scoped to the people assigned to you. There is no leaderboard, no comparison to other buddies, and no score of how you are doing. It is a person's own short to-do list, not a manager's dashboard - which is exactly why it can be all-people without becoming a citywide chase queue.

You set your own capacity, and the escort happens here. Members sit in a pool; a new arrival is auto-assigned to an available buddy who is under their own cap, so the chapter can never silently over-assign. And when you walk a rose into their first GBM and you both sign in, that is the escort the cockpit reads - this desk is where it actually happens; the cockpit only reads it back later.

Audience + design decisions
Who The cockpit's inverse
  • Two audiences, two screens. Leadership gets the cockpit: aggregate funnel health, no individual people, never a chase list.
  • A Rose Buddy gets this desk: only people, only their own few, no rates. Same data, opposite slice.
  • That split is what lets both stay honest at once - process without surveillance up top, people without a citywide queue down here.
Why OK Self-scoped, so not a chase queue
  • This desk is per-person tracking - the thing we exiled from the cockpit. That is fine, because it is self-scoped and self-chosen: your own short list, capped by you.
  • The line we hold: no view ever ranks buddies against each other, and no leadership view ever becomes a per-newcomer list. Individuals live in exactly one place - their own buddy's desk.
Ceiling Availability is the labor ceiling, enforced
  • You set your own cap. The pool can never assign past what each buddy declares, so over-assignment is impossible by construction, not by good intentions.
  • Pause stops new assignments and keeps your current roses. Step out prompts you to hand them off. Either is one tap.
  • When the whole pool is full, new arrivals wait in an unassigned queue the coordinator sees - the signal to grow the pool, never to grind the buddies already in it.
Flow How a rose lands on your desk
  • A new arrival (in the door, any of the four entry doors) is auto-assigned to an available buddy who is under cap - load-balanced to whoever has the fewest.
  • You can also self-assign someone you already know, or release one you cannot serve.
  • A rose rolls off your roster once they reach active - that frees a slot, which is how Tom's slot opened when Elena went active.
Memory Notes travel on handoff
  • What you learn - skills, background, what they want from the org - is the warm intel that later helps place them into the right work.
  • It travels with the person on a handoff, so a buddy going quiet never resets a newcomer to zero. Churn-proof by design.
  • Scope stays tight: you, the next buddy, maybe the coordinator. It is supportive and operational - not an org-wide dossier.
Link The escort happens here, is read there
  • This desk is the prospective side: "you and Maria are both going Thursday - walk in together."
  • The cockpit reads the escort retrospectively, inferred from you both signing in. The optional "log a touch" note the cockpit demoted is written here.
Absent by design
No percentages, rates, or funnel numbers - that is the cockpit's job, not a buddy's. No other buddy's roster, ever. No score of how you are doing, no escort-success scorecard pointed at you - co-attendance makes one computable, and Echo refuses to build it, because it would surveil volunteers. No dues write and no "mark as member" - dues stays read-only from Action Network. No way to assign yourself past your own cap. No citywide list of everyone unwelcomed - that pressure belongs to growing the pool, not to one buddy's screen.
