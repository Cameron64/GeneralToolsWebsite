Cadence cockpit · watch the live cohort

One volunteer watches one live group of ~25-50 people (a cohort - one set of people who joined around the same time) move along the path: in the door → orientation RSVP → oriented → buddy-escorted first GBM → active. This is a review surface, not a chase surface: it makes dormancy visible at a glance - who has gone quiet, who is sitting longer than people usually do at each step. It deliberately does not claim a leak, and it shows no day-90 number: nobody is lost until day 90, so both the leak and the final "stayed in" verdict are read afterward, on the Cohort review page. It never becomes a per-person to-do queue and it assumes no paid staff - one screen, one read-down.

Dues is read-only. Echo reads the dues fact from Action Network by email and never writes it. There is no "mark as member" control anywhere on this screen. A re-reached lapsed person stays a non-member contact until they pay in Action Network.

An automated reminder is not an escort. The reminders fire on their own for everyone on cadence, so "a reminder went out" is never counted as a real touch. A buddy actually walking someone into their first GBM is held apart from the automation everywhere on the screen - and it is read from both people signing in at the same meeting, not from anyone remembering to log it. The cap, the failure line X, and the retention baseline are owned by the membership body; this screen displays and obeys them, and shows them unset until the body sets them.

States + ordering decisions
Frame Two clocks · live vs closed
  • This screen watches a live cohort. Mid-term you can only see where each person sits and how long they have sat there - a snapshot and an aging signal. You cannot see the leak: nobody is lost until day 90.
  • The leak - who actually fell off, and at which step - is only knowable once the cohort closes. That is a different page: Cohort review, the retrospective. The steering wheel lives there, because that is the only place the data is final.
  • So this page does prioritization (who to look at first) but never diagnosis-of-loss. It hands the wheel to the retrospective.
State Stalled vs on cadence
  • Stalled · day 30 (Rosa, Kevin): no engagement in 30 days. Reads as the loudest tag in its row - dormancy made visible. It flags risk, not a loss; nothing is final until day 90.
  • On cadence: dashed, quiet. It is the resting state, not an achievement, so it does not shout.
State No buddy vs assigned
  • No buddy - warm holding (Tom, Kevin, Elena): honest automated-cadence holding, never a fake promise of a buddy. Next action is "Assign buddy."
  • Assigned: shows the buddy's real name. A name here is a relationship promise, not just a reminder.
Data How the escort is known
  • The escort is inferred from sign-in: when the assigned buddy and their newcomer are both signed in at the same first GBM, the escort happened. No one has to remember to log it.
  • This kills the old footgun where a real-but-unlogged escort looked the same as a no-show. The dependency moves from "did a buddy log it" to "was sign-in captured at that meeting."
  • A buddy can still add an optional note ("Log touch") - convenient for jotting how it went, and the one place to correct the inference when co-presence was not a real escort. It is never the source of truth on its own.
  • This assumes the chapter runs a sign-in at GBMs that resolves to the contact record. Echo neither creates nor requires that process - it just reads it where it exists. Low coverage means "we cannot see," never "go run more check-ins."
State First-class doors
  • Paid-first (Tom): dues current, stage still "in the door." A paying member can still be un-welcomed and belongs in the cohort.
  • Reactivation (Aisha): warm lane, formerly lapsed, walks the same spine when they accept the offer. But the open hand holds: a reactivation contact who declines reads as complete · open hand, never "stalled," and never flags for a person.
Column "What happens next" = the machine's voice
  • Auto · the automated cadence has it (a reminder, a check-in). You are reading status, not picking up a task.
  • ⚑ Needs a person · the cadence is out of moves - the column states the situation, it does not give an order. The row's own action button is the bounded write (one of four), and the human chooses whether to use it.
  • Most rows sit at Auto. The few flags are grouped up top so dormancy is easy to find; the aging panel above is the real at-a-glance view.
Order Why this sequence
  • Liveness first so the organizer's own silence is the first thing seen.
  • Triples before the table so no bare number can stand alone.
  • The real escort held apart from the automated reminder - the distinction is the whole argument.
  • The aging panel sits right under the gap it explains: who is parked and what one person could offer - the prospective signal, never a claim of loss. The real leak is retrospective and lives on the Cohort review page.
  • Blind spot shown before the roster so demographics never read as complete.
  • The roster groups the few needs-a-person rows up top but stays mostly "Auto" - a review surface, not a worklist; the aging panel above carries the glance.
Absent by design
No "mark as member" or any dues write. No blended health score that hides the triples. No per-person chase queue or inbox. Nothing that assumes paid staff or a coaching bench. No demographic chart that implies complete data. No number the screen cannot actually observe - an escort is read from both people signing in at the same GBM, not from a manual log, and a meeting with no sign-in captured reads as blind, not as failure. No per-buddy performance rollup or escort-success scorecard - inferring escorts per (buddy, newcomer) pair makes one computable, and Echo deliberately does not build it: it would surveil volunteers. No day-90 number and no leak claim on a live cohort - both are only real once the cohort closes, so they are read on the Cohort review page, never asserted here.
