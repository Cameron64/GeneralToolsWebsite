Pool & program · the steward's view

This is the third leadership screen, and the one that finally has controls. The Cadence cockpit shows leadership whether the welcome is working; the Cohort review shows where it leaked; this panel is the operator's station for the welcoming machine itself - who is in the buddy pool, who has room, who is waiting, and the few exceptions a human has to place by hand. It sits under the same permission as the cockpit, held by several admins, so any steward can act and every action is attributable. Most of the work here is already automatic: ordinary arrivals are matched to a buddy without anyone lifting a finger. What is left for a person is the pool, the capacity, and the exceptions.

Tend the system, not the volunteers. "Sight, not direction" never meant leadership has no controls - it meant two narrower things: do not turn newcomers into a chase list, and do not rank or surveil the buddies. Both still hold here. Every control on this page tends the pool and the flow; the unit of attention is the machine's health, never one buddy's performance. There is no leaderboard, and a reassignment is an act of care for a newcomer, not a mark against a buddy.

Auto by default, hands only on exceptions. Ordinary arrivals auto-assign to an available under-cap buddy, load-balanced. A steward only steps in on what the algorithm cannot do alone: place someone the full pool left waiting, re-home a newcomer whose buddy left without a handoff, or check on a pairing that has stalled. Manual assign and reassign live in one small attention lane - never a roster of every newcomer to work through.

Audience + design decisions
Who The operator of the machine
  • Audience is leadership/admin - the same permission that opens the cockpit, granted to several people. The three leadership screens split by job, not by person.
  • Cockpit asks "is our welcome working?" This asks "is our welcoming machine staffed and flowing?" Different hat, same admin.
  • The pool is shared, not owned by one coordinator. Several stewards can act, so every assign and reassign is attributable.
Why OK Tend the system, not the volunteers
  • Management controls and "sight, not direction" only conflict if you read the rule too widely. It forbids chasing newcomers and grading buddies - not operating the pool.
  • Every control here acts on the pool and the flow. The page never answers "who is the best buddy," and a reassign is framed as care for a newcomer.
  • That is the whole reason this can have controls and the cockpit cannot: it steers the machine, not the people inside it.
Ceiling Capacity is policy and reduce, never conscription
  • Leadership sets the default cap, can lower an individual's effective cap, and can request a raise.
  • It can never force a cap up. Only the buddy raises their own ceiling, from their desk. The labor ceiling survives even with an admin panel.
  • So a full pool is solved by recruiting (region 5), never by quietly cranking everyone's number up.
Flow Auto by default, the queue is the pressure valve
  • Ordinary arrivals auto-assign, load-balanced, with no one lifting a finger. Manual is the exception, kept to one lane.
  • When the pool is full, arrivals wait visibly in the queue instead of silently overloading buddies.
  • Queue depth is the headline number and the grow-the-pool alarm. The system is designed to make a shortage loud, not to absorb it onto the people already volunteering.
Exits Graceful departures never reach here
  • A buddy who steps out warmly uses their desk's handoff - picks a successor or asks the pool to cover. Leadership never touches it.
  • Only an ungraceful exit - ghosting, removal, or leaving with no handoff - orphans a rose into the re-placement lane.
  • Those get a human, by your call, because they carry history. The easy 90% stays automatic.
Memory Notes are shared, and they travel
  • A buddy's notes are org knowledge, not a private file - freely transferable, readable by whoever holds the relationship and by a steward.
  • A steward reads them right here, during a re-placement, so a newcomer never resets to zero when their buddy leaves.
  • The cockpit itself stays people-free; notes surface only where individuals are actually being handled - this page and the buddy's desk.
Absent by design
No per-buddy success rate, no escort scorecard, no ranking - the roster shows availability and load only. No forcing a cap up. No reassign-as-discipline; the framing is always continuity of care. No dues write and no "mark as member" - active is read from Action Network. And no citywide list of individual newcomers as a leadership to-do: the only people on this page are the queue (waiting to be placed) and flagged pairings (a stalled exception). The cohort lives as an aggregate on the cockpit, never as a chase list here.
