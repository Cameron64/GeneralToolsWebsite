Austin DSA · Leadership Brief · For the Steering Committee & Board

Where Our Members Go

Right now a new member's first contact with us is a cold email telling them to go find a Slack channel. No one is at the door. The fix is a warm welcome home: their buddy and their first event from day one. It also lets us finally see where members go.

One direction to bless Read time: ~2 minutes Status: proposed for GBM debate

Build the warm welcome we never had. Seeing the rest comes free.

We already know the widest hole: nobody welcomes new members. We do not need to measure that to justify fixing it. So we build the welcome home first. And because members use it, every login, every RSVP, every hello to their buddy becomes the first real picture of where members go. We get the measurement because members are getting value, not the other way around.

01 The human problem

We abandon people at the front door

Picture the moment a person decides to join. They are hopeful. They want to belong. Here is what we actually do next: we send an email telling them to go find a Slack channel on their own, and the email is written like a warning.

The tone is cloak-and-dagger: guard against bad actors, watch out for doxxing, be careful who you trust. Before anyone has said hello, the chapter's first words to a new comrade are suspicious and cold. Then we leave them to walk themselves in. No one is at the door. No one knows their name. There is no room to enter, just a hallway with a lock on it.

This is the widest hole in the bucket, and we already know it is there. We do not need a study to tell us that dropping a hopeful new member at a locked, paranoid door is how we lose them. The security guidance matters, but it belongs later and in context, after someone feels welcomed. Never as the front door.

Roughly half of last year's joiners are no longer with us. estimate Most did not storm out. They were simply never walked in, then quietly drifted when their dues lapsed.

02 The human fix

A warm welcome home, from day one

Replace the locked hallway with a real room. Right after the national signup email, the new member lands somewhere warm inside Echo, our own chapter site. Not a form. A home.

Who we are

A warm orientation

Plain and human: who we are, what we are fighting for, and where a new person fits. Generous, not bureaucratic, not paranoid.

A real person

Meet your buddy

Every new member is connected to a real comrade, shown by name, with a one-tap way to reach them. No more arriving to an empty room.

Your first event

RSVP in one tap

The next meeting and upcoming events are right there to RSVP, riding the event automation Echo already runs (Zoom, Action Network, and Google Calendar).

The pulse

What's happening

Committees, campaigns, and the next meeting, so a new member can self-orient and find their fit without waiting on an organizer to notice them.

No password

One-tap sign-in

Members sign in with a magic link sent to their email. Nothing to remember, nothing for us to leak. The lowest-friction, smallest-surface door we can build.

Free byproduct

And we can finally see

Because members actually use this home, every login, RSVP, and hello becomes a signal. The picture of where members go falls out for free.

This is one product serving three audiences on one data spine: the welcome home and member home for members, member tracking for organizers, and the leadership dashboard for you. Members log in. Action Network stays the system of record underneath it all, unchanged.

03 The bigger argument

A mass organization needs a mass on-ramp

The cold front door is not one stray email. It is what happens when a chapter builds its welcome for the people already inside it. We are a mass organization with a majoritarian project. We grow only by being legible and welcoming to ordinary working Austinites, including the curious newcomer who has never been to a meeting.

Organizers have a name for the discipline this asks of us: meet the masses where they are. From the masses, to the masses. A formation that can only speak to people already fluent in its subculture is not a vanguard, it is a sect. This is the exact failure Lenin named in "Left-Wing" Communism: an Infantile Disorder: mistaking subcultural purity and in-group comfort for radicalism, and refusing to meet people on terms they can actually enter. Base-building, already our stated theory of change, means rooting in the real working class that exists, not the self-selecting few who already feel at home in left spaces.

Put plainly: you cannot want mass appeal while painting the front of the house in purple polka dots for the in-group and still expect the masses to walk in. Every one of our defaults has a defensible origin. As the mandatory, universal, unexplained thing that greets a stranger at the door, together they work as a filter that quietly shrinks the on-ramp to people who are already comfortable with us. That is a self-inflicted wound for a group whose entire strategy depends on growing.

The exhibit: a real new member's first day

One comrade's actual onboarding the day he joined, 2026-05-27. Five automated touches in about three hours, then silence. Four of the five are not even from us. The one that is, is a security manual.

Not us

National next steps. From DSA National, a "thank you for joining, here are your next steps" email. Every step is national: a national orientation call, a "find your chapter" link, the national publications, a membership card.

Not us

Dues receipt. From DSA National. One line acknowledges us ("you are listed as a member of the Austin chapter"); every call to action after it is national merch, boards, and working groups.

Not us

A Slack auto-invite. From Slack the company. The first local-adjacent touch, but the words are Slack's generic boilerplate about moving conversations out of email. The only human element is that a real person's address triggered it.

Not us

Slack account setup. From Slack the company. Pure product onboarding: connect your tools, fine-tune notifications, install the app. Nothing about Austin DSA, the chapter, any person, or anything happening.

The one from us

A security and conduct manual. The only thing the chapter itself wrote. It opens on adversaries doing harm to our membership, then covers being fired for socialism in Texas, scrubbing your home address off the voter roll and data-broker sites, a full op-sec checklist, infiltrators and covert law enforcement, and a discipline procedure. Our first act of self-expression to a newcomer is a warning.

Then nothing. No dated local invite, no human follow-up, no "your next Austin meeting is here and someone will meet you." It is on the newcomer to find their own way in. Add the lived texture: on our Slack, member names are blacked out, so a newcomer talks blindly in the dark while everyone can see them. And in 2026, with no pandemic emergency, an in-person meeting is a room of near-universal masking with boxes of HEPA filter fans carried through it. To a person walking in for the first time, all of this reads as deeply strange.

The diagnosis is sharp; the prescription is precise. The fix is not to ban masks, drop precautions, or stop protecting comrades who are genuinely at risk. Texas is at-will and people are fired for politics; doxxing is real and the voter roll really does expose home addresses; some comrades are immunocompromised; some face ICE, employer, or landlord exposure that makes caution rational. We already accept that threat model, and we treat op-sec as hygiene, not a veto. Accessibility cuts both ways: the room has to work for the immunocompromised comrade and be legible to the newcomer.

Design for the newcomer

Warmth comes first

Build the first experience for the median curious Austinite, not the median existing member. A human, a welcome, and "here is what's happening and how to come" land before any security or conduct content.

Offer, do not impose

Accommodations, explained

Masking is available and respected. Anonymity is available to those genuinely at risk. Neither is the chapter's chosen face for greeting a stranger. We explain norms in context instead of imposing them as the unexamined default.

Care, not fear

A person at the door

Security guidance is reframed from "guard yourself against us" to "here is how we look out for each other," and it arrives after someone feels they belong. Someone is at the door who knows the newcomer's name.

This is the same warm welcome home from the previous section, stated as doctrine. The welcome home is how we put a person at the door at scale, lead with belonging, and make protection available and easy instead of making fear the welcome mat. Meeting the masses where they are is the whole reason it has to come first.

05 Why we stay flat

The leaky bucket

A steady stream joins. A steady stream drifts away. The bucket never fills, and the widest leak is the one we just named: a new member dropped at a cold door with no one to walk them in.

Inflow

We mostly do not control it. Joins track rent, wages, and ICE pressure, the external surges we cannot manufacture on command.

Outflow

We do control this. Members lapse passively, a quiet dues expiry, not an angry quit. That is exactly why they are recoverable.

Blind

We cannot measure it yet. We have never had the one number: of those who join, what share is still developing at 90 days. The welcome home produces that number as a byproduct.

This is not just our read of Austin. It is the central finding of national's own research. DSA's Growth and Development Committee, in State of DSA 2024-25, studied the whole member roll and found that recruitment rates vary little from chapter to chapter and track outside events, so no chapter has reliably out-recruited its peers through tactics, while retention is the place a chapter can build its own momentum. Nearly half of all members leave within their first year. The bucket above is that finding, drawn for Austin.

06 Current problems

What is broken, named plainly

None of these is a recruiting failure. The cold front door is the first. The rest are places we either cannot see, or cannot afford the labor to act.

No shared map

No definition of the journey

We have never agreed what a member's path looks like, so a QR scan and a developed organizer get counted on the same line.

Invisible

Retention we cannot see

No cohort view, no survival curve. We do not know how many of last year's joiners are still here, or where they stalled.

Structural

The September cliff

A burned-out organizer in an elected role cannot be formally backfilled until the next May convention. Burnout becomes a vacancy that lasts.

Unrecovered

~1,800 lapsed members estimate

Our best estimate from late 2024, not a measured count, and about eighteen months out of date. These are warm contacts: most did not quit, their dues quietly lapsed and no one followed up. Re-winning one costs less organizer time than recruiting a stranger, and today we do nothing with the list.

At the ceiling

Volunteer labor is maxed

A few dozen people do roughly 80% of the work. estimate Any plan that assumes a staffer or a coaching bench we do not have is wrong by definition.

Built, idle

Link Tree underused

Our self-hosted QR and click-tracking tool (Echo) is up and stable, but no one has wired it in. The gap is wiring and ownership, not capability.

07 The plan in one image

Build the welcome first, then let it carry the rest

The welcome home is the first build, not a reward we earn later. It is the known leak, and it self-services: built once, then it walks members in on its own. Reach stays uncapped and free. Deep development is paced to the labor we actually have. The build advances only when a measured fact unlocks the next step, never on a calendar date.

Growth: three co-equal lanes

Reach

Top-of-funnel reach

Tabling, cards, QR, ads, the summer surge. Catches the external waves we cannot create. Every contact lands in Action Network and fires the same-day welcome.

CAP: NONE · ambition allowed
Deep

Deep onboarding cohort

One capped intake cohort gets the full treatment: a buddy, a first-72-hours cadence, a first ask in two weeks, a role by day 90. Pilot one before scaling.

CAP: buddy arithmetic · paced to capacity
Warm

Warm reactivation

Lapsed members re-reached off proven 2025 phone-bank scripts. Its own cohort, its own 90-day number. Prioritized for its low cost per organizer-hour: warm contacts, passive lapses, scripts that already work. The ~1,800 estimate is the motivation, not the business case; the case holds even if the real number is half that.

CAP: paced like the deep cohort
deep_cohort_cap = ( core members willing to buddy × buddy-hours each ) ÷ hours one buddy relationship costs

This is the METHOD, not a number. None of the three inputs is known yet: all are leadership-set or to-be-measured, and they arrive via Ask 2. The formula only shows the cap is derived, not arbitrary. In cohort one the buddies are the existing few-dozen core; the bench is not built yet, so we grow it bottom-up. Honest tradeoff: the welcome home is more software to build up front than a pure read-only spike. It still respects the labor ceiling because it is built once and then self-services, lowering the recurring per-member onboarding labor versus today, where the welcome is manual or simply does not happen. The before-and-after labor ledger is owed at every gate.

Build: six gated stages, each unlocked by a measured fact

PLUMBING0

Prove it and stand up the door

Prove on the live Action Network account that we can read a member's dues status and write a tag back. AND stand up the welcome-home skeleton with magic-link login.

Unlocks whenthe plumbing is proven (read dues, write a tag, on the live Action Network account) and a member can magic-link into a bare welcome home. Proof it works, not a date.
WELCOME1

The warm welcome, for real

A real new-member cohort gets the warm welcome: their buddy, their first event RSVP, the orientation. Value lands immediately, and the first activation numbers fall out as a byproduct.

Unlocks whenthe cohort reaches day 90 and the home has minted our first real activation number, while the labor ledger shows organizer hours per member going down, not up. The job is to produce that first number, not to clear a target; we have never measured this, so there is no fair bar to set yet.
TRUSTWORTHY2

Make it trustworthy

Liveness banner, one batched stalled list, role-realness audit, the retention slot wired to fill later. The byproduct data is made repeatable.

Unlocks whena second cohort runs through the same welcome home and the number is comparable. Now repeatable, not a one-off.
DEEPEN3

Deepen the home

First-72-hours cadence, smarter buddy matching, a morning-of nudge, day-30 stalled detection. The self-service home grows, still one named operator, one screen.

Unlocks whenthe ledger STILL shows hours down with the operator counted, and activation holds. If it needs hand-holding, it is cut.
REACTIVATE4

Open the same door to the lapsed

The same welcome home pointed at the ~1,800 lapsed estimate, with its own baseline and proven scripts. They come back through the same warm front door.

Unlocks whenthe warm cohort clears the bar the GBM sets. Cheapest growth per hour, so it earns priority, not appendix status.
SCALE5

The snowflake turns

Core detection, net-active, the 12-month retention curve, larger or parallel deep cohorts.

Unlocks wheneach new deep cohort is sized by buddy arithmetic and the prior cohort cleared its bar. The bench is the Core we earned.

Each stage ships on a measured gate, never a calendar date. This is a gated sequence, not a Gantt chart. The welcome home is built first because it is the known leak and it self-services; reach stays uncapped at every stage; only deep development is paced.

08 The guardrails

How we keep from painting ourselves in

One hard constraint, two non-negotiables that can veto any design, and two tradeoffs we accept with eyes open.

Cannot be crossed

!
Never abandon Action NetworkAction Network stays the system of record for identity and dues. The welcome home reads people and dues from it by email and mirrors back only a coarse, low-sensitivity tag. Magic-link login is an added door onto that record, never a replacement for it.
1
Volunteer-labor ceilingEvery design must reduce net organizer hours. We never assume a staffer or a coaching bench we do not have. The thin core is protected as a hard cap, because of the September cliff.
2
Democratic sovereigntySoftware is staff, not a decider. Tools inform and execute the membership's decisions; they never make a call the GBM reserved to itself.

Chosen with eyes open

A
Data is treated as an assetWe collect and use member data rather than minimize it, because you cannot develop people you cannot see. We name the threat plainly (ICE, employers, landlords) and apply op-sec hygiene as a control, not a veto.
B
Technology may be load-bearingReal dependencies are allowed, no manual fallback required. The price is that named ownership and operability must carry the weight, tied straight back to the labor ceiling.

A system nobody can run violates the labor ceiling, however impressive. That is why ownership, not a manual fallback, is the safeguard.

11 Why this is safe

No big bet. Nothing irreversible.

Every step earns the next. We deliver real value first, the warm welcome, and the measurement we have never had falls out as a byproduct.

01
Prove the plumbing before we cost anything. A small technical check confirms the Action Network path is real and stands up a bare welcome home, before a single estimate or commitment is made.
02
Then one welcome-home cohort. A real new-member cohort, not the whole list. It delivers the warm welcome AND mints the activation baseline we have never had.
03
Scale only on a measured gate. Each stage unlocks on a confirmed fact, and only when the labor ledger shows organizer hours going down, not up.
04
Leadership keeps every reserved decision. The cap, the failure threshold, the baselines stay yours. The tool surfaces options and executes your choices; it never decides where the chapter gives up.

The deepest failure mode we are guarding against is the one named twice in the strategy: a surge auto-welcomed, then quietly lapsed a year later. The deep cap and the gates exist precisely to prevent it.

One of three strategy assets for Austin DSA. Proposed input to a debate the GBM owns; not ratified. On headcount: the chapter has two different-vintage estimates of good-standing membership, from two different sources, and they do not agree. We anchor on the 2024-11-11 LC figure of ~887 dues-current paired with ~1,800 lapsed, the same pair the binding-constraint argument cites. A separate MEC-notes snapshot puts members in good standing (MIGs) at ~1,430. Both denote roughly the same concept, both are point-in-time estimates, neither is a live count. The honest answer to "so which is it" is that the gap is exactly what the Stage 0 spike resolves, by replacing both with one live Action Network pull at pilot start. Every percentage, every cohort size, every timing window, and the failure threshold X shown here are example or placeholder values, not measured results. The whole point of this initiative is that we cannot yet see these numbers.

Evidentiary basis.
